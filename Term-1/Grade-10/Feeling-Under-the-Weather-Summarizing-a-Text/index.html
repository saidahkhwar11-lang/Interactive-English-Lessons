ACTIVITY 1 · Starter
1 · Starter · 5 min · Pair

What Makes a Good Summary?

Student instruction: Discuss with your partner: What should we do before we summarize a difficult paragraph?
What can help us understand unfamiliar words without immediately using a dictionary?
✎ Use context clues. Read the words and sentences around the unfamiliar word and look for definitions, examples, synonyms, contrasts, or explanations.
ACTIVITY 2 · Context Clues
2 · Model · 6 min · Whole Class

Step 1 — Context Clues FIRST

Student instruction: Read Paragraph A. Highlight unfamiliar words. Use the clues around each word to figure out its meaning before summarizing.

A   Weather has a powerful impact on the physical world. It also affects people’s personalities. How do we know about the effects of weather on people? We know from biometeorologists. These scientists study weather. They study how atmospheric conditions affect human health and emotions. The word atmosphere means “the air around the earth.” “Atmospheric conditions at a time or place” is a definition of the word weather. Some examples of these conditions are sun, wind, rain, snow, humidity (the amount of moisture in the air), and air pressure (the force of air). The weather conditions of the atmosphere greatly influence (or affect) people’s health, thinking, and feelings.

✎ humidity → “the amount of moisture in the air”
✎ air pressure → “the force of air”
✎ influence → “or affect”
✎ atmosphere → “the air around the earth”

These definitions and synonyms are written directly in the paragraph.
ACTIVITY 3 · Find Main Idea
3 · Model · 4 min · Whole Class

Step 2 — Find the Main Idea

Student instruction: Now that the vocabulary is clearer, ask: What is Paragraph A mainly explaining?

Weather has a powerful impact on the physical world. It also affects people’s personalities. How do we know about the effects of weather on people? We know from biometeorologists. These scientists study weather. They study how atmospheric conditions affect human health and emotions. The word atmosphere means “the air around the earth.” “Atmospheric conditions at a time or place” is a definition of the word weather. Some examples of these conditions are sun, wind, rain, snow, humidity (the amount of moisture in the air), and air pressure (the force of air). The weather conditions of the atmosphere greatly influence (or affect) people’s health, thinking, and feelings.

✎ Main idea: Weather and atmospheric conditions can affect people’s health, thinking, emotions, and feelings.
ACTIVITY 4 · Select Key Details
4 · Model · 4 min · Whole Class

Step 3 — Select Important Details

Student instruction: Which details are important enough to keep? Do not copy every sentence.
Paragraph A explains what biometeorologists study and gives examples of atmospheric conditions.
✎ Biometeorologists study how weather affects people.
✎ Atmospheric conditions include sun, wind, rain, snow, humidity and air pressure.
✎ These conditions can influence health, thinking and feelings.
ACTIVITY 5 · Model Summary
5 · Model · 4 min · Whole Class

Step 4 — Summarize Paragraph A

Student instruction: Combine the main idea and the most important details into a short summary in your own words.
Useful summarizing phrases:
Overall, ...   •   The paragraph mainly explains ...   •   In particular, ...   •   The key point is ...   •   In short, ...
✎ Model summary:

Overall, the paragraph explains how weather can affect people as well as the physical world. In particular, biometeorologists study how atmospheric conditions, such as humidity and air pressure, influence human health, thinking, and emotions. In short, different weather conditions can have a significant effect on how people feel and function.
ACTIVITY 6 · Your Paragraph

ACTIVITY 7 · Summarize in Notebook
7 · Main Activity · 9 min · Individual

Summarize Your Paragraph

Student instruction: After solving the unfamiliar vocabulary, summarize your paragraph in your notebook.
1. Write the Main Idea.
2. Select the Important Details.
3. Write a Short Summary in Your Own Words.
Notebook routine:
Context Clues → Main Idea → Key Details → Short Summary
Which details can you remove without changing the paragraph’s main message?
ACTIVITY 8 · Group Share
8 · Group Share · 5 min · Group

Share Your Summary

Student instruction: Take turns sharing. Explain one new word you figured out from context and then share your paragraph summary.
Use this speaking frame:

“One unfamiliar word was __________. The context helped me understand that it means __________.”

“My paragraph mainly explains __________.”
“Overall, __________.”
