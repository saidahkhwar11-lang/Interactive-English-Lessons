33 mins Main Activity 1 (7 min) – Page 9, Activity 2: Vocabulary & Audio
Teacher introduces deadline, cross it off, schedule, on my own, tip, tutor, manage. Play Audio 1 once for listening only, then replay and pause after each item for repetition. Students match words to meanings, answer seven quick-check questions, and create two short examples with a partner. Answers are revealed one at a time.

Main Activity 2 (8 min) – Page 10, Activity 3: Fill in the Gaps
Students read the seven definitions, then complete sentences A–G independently using context clues. They compare answers with a partner before checking. Teacher uses the single sequential answer-reveal control and asks students to explain the clue that helped them choose selected words. Expected answers: A manage; B schedule; C on my own; D deadline; E tip; F tutor; G cross it off.

Main Activity 3 (18 min) – Page 11, Activity 4: Listening for Main Ideas & Specific Information
First listening: Students close the book and listen to Audio 2 for gist. They identify Terry Sargent, the purpose of the workshop, and the three main tips: use a calendar/daily planner; make a daily to-do list; learn to say no.
Second listening: Students listen again and add details. Tip 1: write classes, social plans, exercise/sports, appointments, deadlines and meetings; set phone reminders. Tip 2: start with important tasks, keep goals reasonable, cross off finished items, and begin the next day's list. Tip 3: say no to distractions when necessary so you can follow your plan.
Comprehension: Students answer additional questions about why college time management is different, what belongs in a planner, how reminders help, and why saying no matters. Answers reveal sequentially.
Deep Thinking: Students choose the tip most useful for Grade 10 and support their answer with one detail from the listening and one realistic action they can start this week.

Teacher Role: Model, play/replay audio, question, scaffold note-taking, reveal answers, and provide feedback.
Student Role: Listen actively, repeat vocabulary, complete tasks, take notes, compare with peers, justify answers, and reflect on personal time management.

Groups evaluate five study habits: using a to-do list, making time for friends, setting deadline reminders, studying all night, and taking a refreshing break.
