Main 1 · Retrieval Check: Prepositions of Place (10 min) · Page 18.
Purpose: Confirm that students can independently transfer the location language learned in Parts 1–2 into accurate sentences before writing their own email.
Strategy: Individual retrieval → evidence check → pair justification. Teacher Role: Begin without reteaching; ask students to locate the family apartment and complete the map sentences independently. Circulate and record common misconceptions without immediately correcting them. Prompt students with “What on the map proves your answer?” and “Which relationship do you see?” After independent thinking, allow brief pair comparison, then reveal answers one by one using the handwritten annotations. Use errors to decide whether a 1-minute clarification is needed before writing. Student Role: Read each clue, locate both places, select the precise preposition, justify the choice from the map and self-correct after reveal. Early finishers create two new accurate location sentences that could be used in an email. AFL / Evidence: five independent responses, oral justification, teacher observation of misconceptions, self-correction after reveal and two extension sentences.
Main 2 · Mastery Check: Email Language and Structure (10 min) · Page 19.
Purpose: Check whether students now recognize the language choices needed to construct a complete friendly email without relying on the previous lessons.
Strategy: Think–Sort–Justify–Correct. Teacher Role: Present the phrases without first reviewing the answers. Ask students to classify them as Greetings, Opening Statements, Closing Statements or Signatures and require a reason for selected choices. Probe understanding with questions such as “Could this phrase open the email? Why?” and “What should come immediately before the signature?” Invite pairs to compare classifications, then reveal each category progressively. Highlight that writers choose suitable phrases rather than copying every example. Student Role: Categorize the phrases, explain their function, compare with a partner, correct misconceptions and select one opening and one closing they could realistically use in their own email. AFL / Evidence: category accuracy, quality of justification, peer correction and students’ ability to select appropriate language for an authentic email.
Main 3 · Independent Performance Task: Write Your Email (15 min).
Live Writing Tools: 👩‍🎓 Student Interactive Writing Page   |   📺 Teacher Live Board
Students write and submit on the interactive page while the teacher monitors responses live on the board during the independent performance task.

Purpose: This is the culminating check after An Email from College – Parts 1 and 2. Students demonstrate that they can independently plan and produce an email using the structure, vocabulary and language previously taught.
Strategy: Independent application / gradual release (“You do”). Teacher Role: Give the writing purpose and audience, remind students only of the success criteria, and avoid composing sentences for them. Ask students to choose a meaningful topic (college/campus, neighborhood, a place visited, new friends or classes), make a quick paragraph plan, then write. During circulation, conference through questions rather than answers: “What is the topic of this paragraph?”, “Where is your supporting detail?”, “Which Chapter 1 word can strengthen this idea?”, “Can your reader picture the location?” and “Have you closed the email appropriately?” Direct students back to the model only when they need to self-check structure. Student Role: Independently select audience/topic; plan a logical sequence; write Greeting + Opening, one focused body paragraph, Closing + Signature; include relevant Chapter 1 vocabulary and at least two accurate prepositions of place; reread for clarity and conventions. AFL / Evidence: teacher conference notes plus the actual student email as performance evidence. Look specifically for correct format, paragraph unity, relevant details, vocabulary transfer, accurate prepositions, sentence clarity and appropriate closing.
35 min
Plenary / Exit TicketFinal Proof of Learning – Peer / Self Assessment (5 min): Students first reread their own email against the success criteria, then exchange with a partner. The reviewer checks: complete email structure; one clear topic per paragraph; relevant supporting details; appropriate Chapter 1 vocabulary; accurate prepositions of place; capitals, spelling and punctuation; and an appropriate closing/signature. The reviewer gives one specific strength + one actionable improvement. The writer makes one final correction and answers: “What can I now do independently when writing an email?” Teacher Role: sample-check completed emails and use the evidence to identify students ready to move on and students needing a short writing conference/reteach. AFL: completed email + checklist + peer comment + final student correction/reflection.5 min
