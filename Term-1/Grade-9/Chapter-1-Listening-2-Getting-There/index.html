Listen, trace, explain

Pairs
1 · Successful result

Dialogue 1. The listener understands the route to the bus station.

2 · Unsuccessful result

Dialogue 2. The man is still unsure how to reach the police station.

3 · Helpful or confusing language

Helpful: Dialogue 1 gives exact street names, two blocks, the traffic lights and clear landmarks. Confusing: Dialogue 2 says “a few blocks,” refers to a road fork and gives an unnamed restaurant as a landmark.

4 · What went wrong?

The distances and landmarks were vague, and the man mixed up College Road with College Lane. Better directions would name each street and turn, give an exact block count, and check that he understood.

Activities 2 + 3 · pages 14–15

Complete the dialogue and compare methods

Pairs → groups
Fill the gaps, follow the route on the map, then listen and check.
Start at “You Are Here” ↓

Woman 1: Hi, sorry to bother you, but (1) can you tell me where the bus station is please?

Woman 2: (2) Of course. Go straight along this road – Pacific Drive – for two blocks.

(3) Turn right at the traffic lights onto Olive Tree Road.

Woman 1: Can I (4) just check something? When I turn right after two blocks, I will be on Olive Tree Road, or is Olive Tree Road after two blocks?

Woman 2: You will be on Olive Tree Road when you turn right on the second street from here. Go along Olive Tree Road for one block and, after the shopping mall, turn left onto 24th Street. The bus station (5) is on your right, opposite a hotel and (6) next to a car park.

Woman 2: Could you (7) please explain the second part?

Woman 1: Of course. The bus station is on your right, when you are on 24th Street. There is a hotel on your left and (8) just after the bus station, there is a car park. So if you see lots of cars, but no buses, you know you have gone too far!

Exit ticket · page 15

Choose the best way to get there

Open discussion
In small groups, discuss three situations. Say which way of getting directions is best and explain why. Respond politely to someone else’s idea.
Try these situations

① You are visiting a new city.

② Your phone battery is empty.

③ You are driving to an unfamiliar place.

Speak like this

“I think that … because …”

“What about …?”

“I see your point, but …”

Exit check: I named a method, gave a reason, and replied politely.

Getting There · Lesson Plan

Al Reyadah School · Grade 9 General · Teacher: Ms. Saidah Khwar · 45 minutes

Objectives: Follow spoken directions on a map; use expressions to agree, suggest and disagree politely; compare ways to get directions with reasons.

Success criteria: Trace and explain a route; complete and check the dialogue; give a reasoned opinion and respond politely.

Keywords: directions, block, traffic lights, opposite, turn left/right, clarify, repeat, GPS, street map.

H.O.T. question: Why can an instruction sound correct yet still lead someone to the wrong place?
