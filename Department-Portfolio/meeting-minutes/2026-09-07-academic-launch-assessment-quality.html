English Department Meeting Minutes

Date: Monday, 7 September 2026
September Theme: Academic Launch and Strengthening the Quality of Assessment

1. General Objective

  • Unify academic expectations for September across departments.
  • Ensure high-quality teaching and learning inside classrooms.
  • Apply student assessment policy fairly, accurately, and consistently.
  • Clarify coordinators’ responsibilities in monitoring, support, and raising student achievement.

2. Academic Launch Expectations

  • Classroom teaching and learning remain the first priority.
  • Teachers must follow the approved curriculum map and pacing plan.
  • Lesson titles and learning outcomes should be visible and clear in every lesson, including cover classes.
  • Lesson time should be used fully for purposeful learning.
  • Teaching strategies should address different student levels and needs.
  • Teachers should maintain a safe, organized, and motivating classroom environment.
  • Coordinators are expected to lead academically, monitor quality, support teachers, analyse results, and intervene early when gaps appear.

3. Lesson Planning and Professional Readiness

  • Ensure all term and weekly plans are complete and aligned with the approved timeline.
  • Confirm that learning outcomes and target skills are clear.
  • Prepare teaching resources before lesson delivery.
  • Include differentiation, remedial activities, and enrichment opportunities.
  • Keep department electronic files organized and updated.

4. Teacher Performance Follow-up

  • Conduct short, regular classroom walkthroughs.
  • Provide specific and actionable feedback.
  • Support new teachers and identify professional development needs.
  • Share effective teaching practices across the department.
  • Address issues within the department first and escalate when an administrative decision is required.
  • Document visits, actions, and the impact of recommendations.

5. Attendance, Behaviour and Student Support

  • Record student attendance in Al Manhal within the required time.
  • If the system is unavailable, keep a temporary paper/electronic record and update the system later.
  • Follow up frequent absences and lateness with social workers.
  • Prevent absence and lateness from causing learning loss.
  • Monitor student discipline, uniform, and expected behaviour.
  • Report repeated concerns early for intervention.

6. National Education Charter

  • Integrate the Charter values into daily teaching practices.
  • Connect values with classroom situations, homework, and activities.
  • Promote responsibility, discipline, respect, and national identity.
  • Clarify the family’s role in supporting student learning and commitment.

7. Student Assessment Policy

  • Assessment should measure learning outcomes, identify strengths and needs, improve teaching, provide feedback, and guide evidence-based decisions.
  • Use diagnostic assessment at the start of the year or unit to identify learning gaps.
  • Use continuous formative assessment through questioning, observation, short tasks, worksheets, discussion, exit tickets, projects, peer assessment, and self-assessment.
  • Use summative assessment after defined learning outcomes have been taught, following approved regulations and weightings.

8. Quality Requirements for Assessment Tools

  • Assess only learning outcomes that have actually been taught.
  • Match student age, level, and available assessment time.
  • Include varied levels of difficulty and thinking.
  • Use clear instructions and accurate language.
  • Provide clear marking schemes or rubrics.
  • Apply approved accommodations for students of determination.
  • Review and moderate tools before use.

9. Fairness, Marking and Feedback

  • Unify assessment standards across teachers of the same grade and subject.
  • Do not award marks without clear evidence of learning.
  • Do not use marks as punishment or behaviour control.
  • Maintain confidentiality and avoid bias.
  • Document any grade adjustment and its reason.
  • Use unified answer keys or marking criteria.
  • Provide clear feedback showing what students achieved and what they need to improve.
  • Review marking samples within the department to ensure consistency.

10. Grade Entry, Analysis and Early Intervention

  • Enter grades within official deadlines and review them before approval.
  • Ensure there are no missing or unjustified marks.
  • Match recorded marks with assessment evidence.
  • Keep assessment records and evidence organized.
  • Coordinators should review samples of each teacher’s grade records.
  • After each assessment, analyse results by class, section, and learning outcome.
  • Identify students below expectations and learning outcomes showing collective weakness.
  • Prepare short targeted support plans and enrichment for high achievers.
  • Reassess the impact of intervention and escalate cases requiring additional support.

11. Coordinator Responsibilities

  • Explain the assessment policy to all department teachers.
  • Ensure teachers access the approved official policy.
  • Prepare a clear department assessment plan.
  • Standardize tools and marking criteria when required.
  • Moderate assessments before implementation.
  • Review student work and marking samples.
  • Monitor grade entry deadlines and accuracy.
  • Analyse results and submit a brief report on actions taken.
  • Follow up remedial plans and measure their impact.
  • Document department meetings and decisions.
  • Report any issue that may affect fairness or reliability.

12. Decisions and Recommendations

  1. Discuss the student assessment policy in department meetings and document staff acknowledgement.
  2. No assessment tool is to be administered before department review and moderation.
  3. Complete diagnostic assessment analysis and prepare support plans.
  4. Conduct regular classroom visits focusing on learning outcomes and formative assessment quality.
  5. Meet all official grade-entry deadlines and review marks before approval.
  6. Standardize marking criteria and ensure fairness across classes and sections.
  7. Submit a short end-of-September report covering achievements, challenges, and corrective actions.
  8. Refer any case or procedure not covered by policy to school administration before taking action.

Closing Note

Success in September will be measured by the impact of our work on student learning, not by the number of activities completed. Coordinators are expected to leave the meeting with a clear departmental plan, follow implementation through documented evidence, and raise challenges early before they become learning gaps.
